THE ACTUAL ARTICLE

I have been pretty plus-sized for most of my life. Saggy man boobs, bulging tummy, an ass fat enough to have both late bloomers and the poster children of prediabetes and sarcopenia in jealous heaving frenzies, and chubby cheeks — the face kind. In the last year, however, I have also gotten lean enough to see my abs for the first time in my life. I can confirm two things.

1. Losing weight and gaining muscle doesn't make you less depressed. It does make you considerably more attractive, though. So maybe you can claim you are more confident — but that isn't exactly happiness. "Well, Jed, some people are depressed because of their weight." We will get into that shortly.

2. The women who serve food actually give you less meat when you are overweight. I am not even joking — this is a grave injustice and I stand vehemently against it. This has happened to me so frequently, and I am starting to sense it applies to more than just protein. The last time, the server gave me less groundnuts and plantain chips than the guy beside me. What the actual fuck? And I will be weird if I ask for more. The fat guy wants more food, ahahaha. (Which direction am I going in?)

Anyway, I "let myself go" earlier this year, and I have been wondering why. (Honestly, I haven't been wondering. I am getting constantly harassed by family and friends who feel the need to make me uncomfortable in the skin I wake up in every day. As if I can't feel my belly roll when I sit. As if I can't feel that my clothes are a bit tighter. As if I don't wake up every morning, look at myself in the mirror, and see that I've gained weight. How utterly rude.) I have been skirting around multiple potential answers.

1. I just don't feel like it anymore.
2. Since I realized I could lose weight and it wasn't too hard, I had lost enthusiasm.
3. I have lost my passion because I hit most of the goals I set, and I hit them a bit too easily.
4. I can't afford the good gym anymore, and going back to the bad gym is such a big downgrade that I would just rather not. I don't like the bad gym.

But I think the more honest answer is probably more along the lines of: I am depressed and don't really want to do the things I love anymore. In this article, dead men refers to depressed people. Because duh.

FOOD IS THE OPIUM OF THE MASSES

This part is short. Food is accessible and makes dopamine easily accessible. And that should be the end of it. But it's deeper than that. Everything is deeper when it's in my hands.

When Karl Marx said religion was the opium of the masses, he wasn't just saying it as an insult — at least, I don't think so. He was saying that religion also alleviated some of the pain the masses went through, placated them, made them a bit better equipped to deal with societal alienation by numbing them. Ironically, it also meant they were less likely to fight oppressive structures on earth, because there was a better world waiting in the afterlife. The solution was the problem — for them and for their children.

See the parallels to food? Only me? Damn. (I must be crazy.)

In the modern world, food is more accessible than it has ever been. Mega-corporations produce processed food en masse. Capitalism makes bad food cheaper than vegetables, and so on and so forth. It provides temporary comfort for all the struggles and mental duress a person goes through daily — work stress, school, boredom, everything. (My mouth gets lonely very often.) As human beings, we quite literally cannot sustain anger while eating. If you wanted to stop an argument, walk between the people arguing while eating. I'm not kidding — Google it. For a bigger person, this ease of access is a double-edged sword. It means that after getting bullied, after feeling horrible about body image, they can just get a big meal and drown their sorrows in a guilty pleasure. Yale estimates that 20 percent of people are addicted to food. Food is an ouroboros: eat more, need more — whole time, the world gets worse around you.

WHAT HAS THIS MEANT?

Your fat is a veil that covers your failing mental health.

People attack your fatness before truly meeting you. You yourself have learned to mask it rather well. Fat people become the funny friend, because any expression of emotion makes them a fridge protecting snacks — makes them too much, literally. So you build a personality for so long, and even though you are too much, you are simultaneously never enough. Whole time, your bad mental health burns a hole through you. Your fatness is more important than your depression, and the moment you stop performing — stop cracking self-deprecating jokes, stop laughing at the ones where you are subtly the punchline — you become a nervous child, walking around in a shirt a size too small, hands tucked under it to hide your pudgy belly.

I'm amused that no one has asked why I stopped going to the gym and immediately put on 10kg in three months. Surely, said like that, it is obvious that something drastic must be wrong. But alas — your mother would rather tell you to lose weight than interrogate what makes a man quit after four years of trying to be handsome.
