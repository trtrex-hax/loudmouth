---
title: "On why I stopped explaining my poems before people read them"
date: "2026-04-20"
type: "rant"
excerpt: "A poem with a disclaimer is already apologizing for itself."
---

I used to do this thing where before someone read one of my poems I would say something like — "it's about my grandmother, but not really, it's more about the idea of inheritance, and also the sea is a metaphor" — and then hand it over.

I thought I was being helpful. Context, right? Framing. Setting them up to get it.

What I was actually doing was apologizing in advance. I was saying: *I'm not sure this works on its own, so here is a key for the parts that don't.*

A poem that needs a paragraph of explanation before the first line is a poem that doesn't trust itself. And if the poet doesn't trust it, why would the reader?

The explanation is also a kind of control. You're telling the reader how to read it before they've had the chance to bring themselves to it. You're closing off the meaning before it's had time to breathe.

The best thing a poem can do is surprise you with a feeling you didn't know you were carrying. You can't do that if you've already told the reader what to feel.

So I stopped. I hand them the poem. I watch their face. I let them have whatever they're going to have.

It's harder. It requires believing the work is enough. Some days I believe that. Some days I don't. But either way, the explanation stays in my mouth.
